## 1. Responsibility and the limits of a perimeter

Imagine the university notes app runs on managed infrastructure. Microsoft operates its underlying platform, but our team still decides who can see unpublished exam solutions. Responsibility shifts with the service model; outsourcing infrastructure does not outsource every decision about identities, data, configuration, and use.

Defence in depth combines layers: permissions, device controls, network boundaries, application checks, encryption, monitoring, and response. A single successful control does not prove every layer works. Zero Trust adds a useful habit: verify the request, limit its privileges, and plan for compromise. Being on campus Wi-Fi should not grant unrestricted access. [Zero Trust reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security/zero-trust/zero-trust-overview).

## 2. Who is connecting, and what may they do?

Authentication establishes an identity; authorisation decides whether that identity can perform an action. A successful sign-in can therefore be followed by an access denial. Record the identity, resource, action, and scope before changing permissions.

Entra ID represents people and workloads. Employees, guests, applications, service principals, and managed identities have different uses and lifecycles. A managed identity lets a supported workload obtain tokens without our application storing a client secret; it still needs permissions. Entra directory roles and Azure resource roles govern different things. An administrator of users does not automatically become a reader of every student's document. Review [identity notes](../../en/06-identity.md) for those boundaries.

## 3. Credentials, MFA, hashing, and encryption

Two passwords are still one kind of factor. MFA combines different factor categories, but methods do not offer equal resistance to phishing. For privileged access, assess supported phishing-resistant methods and recovery procedures rather than assuming every second prompt gives identical protection. User registration, device support, and actual policy enforcement matter.

Encryption protects data using keys and supports recovery of the original content when authorised. A cryptographic hash produces a digest used for comparisons; it is not reversible encryption. A matching file digest is evidence of matching content under the chosen assumptions, not proof of its author. Password storage requires an appropriate password-hashing design, including salts and work factors; these notes are not a recipe to build an authentication system.

## 4. Conditional Access is a decision policy

Conditional Access combines signals with access controls. For example, a finance application could require an appropriate authentication strength and a compliant device. Describe the targeted identities, application, conditions, grant controls, and exclusions. A policy aimed at one application is not evidence that all applications are protected.

The policy does not replace application permissions or a denial-of-service protection service. For a proposed rollout, use a hypothetical pilot and report-only analysis; evaluate expected allowed and blocked cases, administrator recovery, and dependencies. This guide does not instruct you to enable a tenant-wide rule. [Conditional Access reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/conditional-access/overview).

## 5. Privilege, reviews, and identity risk

PIM addresses privileged access that should be eligible or time limited instead of permanently active. Activation can involve approval, justification, and authentication requirements. Eligibility is not proof that the role is active, and time limitation does not make an excessively broad role appropriate.

Access reviews ask whether an existing entitlement is still needed. ID Protection identifies identity-related risk signals; a risk detection is evidence to investigate, not a complete explanation of every incident. These tools can work together, but they answer different questions: who may activate, who should retain access, and which identity behaviour looks risky? [PIM reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/id-governance/privileged-identity-management/pim-configure).

## 6. Azure security needs several layers

A network security group filters relevant network traffic. Azure Firewall, web application firewalls, and DDoS protection have different purposes; none automatically grants correct application authorisation. A private endpoint changes the network path, while DNS and identity controls still require their own configuration.

Defender for Cloud helps assess cloud security posture and offers workload-protection capabilities through applicable plans. A recommendation means a condition deserves assessment; clicking a product on does not prove remediation. For our notes app, distinguish an exposed administrative port, an overprivileged identity, and an application that returns another student's file. Each needs different evidence and a responsible owner.

## 7. Detecting and responding across products

Defender XDR correlates signals across supported, onboarded Microsoft security products to help investigate related activity. It is useful when an email, identity event, and endpoint alert might be parts of one attack. It cannot correlate a sensor that has never sent the necessary data. [Defender XDR reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-xdr/microsoft-365-defender).

Sentinel provides SIEM capabilities for collecting and analysing security data across sources, with automation and orchestration options for response. SIEM concerns information and event management; SOAR concerns coordinated response workflows. Define connectors, rules, retention, permissions, and response ownership. An automatic account-disable action needs more care than an analyst notification. [Sentinel reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/sentinel/overview).

## 8. Purview: distinguish protection from evidence

A sensitivity label classifies content and can apply configured protection where supported. DLP evaluates sensitive-data handling and can warn or restrict matching activities. A label called Confidential does not, by its name alone, block every external share.

Retention governs keeping or deleting content according to applicable rules. Audit records activity; eDiscovery helps find, preserve, and review information for an investigation. Neither is a backup strategy. Compliance Manager can organise improvement actions and assessments, but a dashboard score is not a legal determination of compliance. Start with a concrete data obligation, owner, scope, and evidence. [Purview overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/purview).

## 9. A practical exercise without changing a tenant

Draw a fictional flow: a guest downloads course notes, an editor uploads a solution, and an administrator manages access. Use synthetic document names and no personal information. Mark trust boundaries and identify one threat at each step.

For each threat, write a preventive control, a detection signal, an owner, and a recovery action. Add an expected failure: a guest should not download an unpublished solution. Explain whether a denied request, an audit event, or an alert would support your conclusion. These are expected outcomes, not observed Azure results. Deliver the diagram and a six-row evidence table; do not enable policies or deploy services for this exercise.
