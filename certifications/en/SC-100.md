# SC-100 · Microsoft Cybersecurity Architect

[Certifications](README.md) · [Ten cases](SC-100-cases.md) · [Workbook](SC-100-workbook.md) · [Español](../es/SC-100.md)

SC-100 asks me to connect security decisions to business outcomes. Our fictional university notes app now spans campus infrastructure, Azure, another cloud, Microsoft 365, and an AI study assistant. Buying controls is easier than showing who owns each risk and how the whole service remains safe and recoverable.

Reference checked **2026-11-01**: [official study guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/sc-100), objectives effective **2026-10-21**.

|---|---|
| Security best practices and priorities | 20–25% |
| Security operations, identity, and compliance capabilities | 25–30% |
| Infrastructure security | 25–30% |

The [Cybersecurity Architect Expert credential](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/cybersecurity-architect-expert/) requires SC-100 and **at least one** of Identity and Access Administrator Associate, Security Operations Analyst Associate, or Cloud and AI Security Engineer Associate. It does not require all three. Recheck the credential before planning your route.

These original notes and offline exercises support study; they are not official questions, complete objective coverage, executed cloud changes, or a promise of passing. Localised exam updates may follow a different schedule.

## 1. Begin with critical services and accountable owners

I would first identify what the university must protect: published notes, unreleased assessment answers, student records, and the ability to teach during an incident. Classify business criticality separately from technical resource size. A small identity dependency can interrupt every course.

For each threat, record affected operations, impact, likelihood assumptions, existing controls, residual risk, and a named risk owner. A security team can recommend controls; the authorised business owner accepts remaining exposure. Define measurable acceptance criteria, such as recovering authorised document reads within the agreed time. Record decisions and rejected alternatives in an [architecture decision record](ADR-template.md). A product name or dashboard score cannot replace this argument.

## 2. Use reference frameworks for different decisions

[MCRA](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security/adoption/mcra) helps map security capabilities and their integration across the estate. [MCSB](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/security/benchmark/azure/overview) helps translate those capabilities into control requirements and implementation guidance. Neither supplies our university's priorities automatically.

Use [CAF landing zones](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cloud-adoption-framework/ready/landing-zone/) to allocate platform guardrails and workload responsibilities. Use the [Well-Architected security guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/security/) to review workload decisions and their tradeoffs. Build a coverage table: requirement, control, scope, owner, evidence, and gap. An inherited policy might govern production subscriptions while leaving a departmental SaaS service outside its scope. Document that boundary rather than claiming enterprise coverage from one successful assessment.

## 3. Make Zero Trust cross every boundary

A campus network connection does not establish trust. Evaluate identity, device, resource, request context, and application authorisation while applying least privilege and assuming compromise. Network segmentation reduces reachable paths; it does not decide whether a student may read another faculty's restricted material.

Draw trust boundaries around the browser, API, document store, administrative plane, external collaborator, and AI retrieval service. Distinguish authentication from authorisation and data-plane access from resource administration. Explain where Conditional Access, application permissions, endpoint posture, and network controls contribute. For each important request, describe an expected allow and denial, the evidence required, and a failure mode. Availability and administrator recovery also belong in the access design.

## 4. Protect human, workload, and agent privilege

Separate normal users, external researchers, administrators, deployment pipelines, indexers, and agents. Propose scoped roles, time-limited privileged activation, protected administration devices, access reviews, and emergency access with monitoring. A compromised campus directory can affect cloud recovery, so include hybrid identity dependencies and hardening requirements.

Prefer platform-managed credentials or federation where suitable, with explicit resource permissions and lifecycle owners. An agent identity, including applicable Microsoft Entra Agent ID capabilities, needs bounded tools, access policies, and accountability; naming an agent does not authorise every action. Record licence and feature assumptions. Keep design-level permission requirements here; use [SC-300](SC-300.md) to study the detailed identity administration decisions.

## 5. Design a SOC capability with response authority

Microsoft Defender XDR and Microsoft Sentinel can contribute detection, correlation, investigation, and response. Architecture must define which campus, cloud, identity, endpoint, and SaaS signals actually arrive, their latency, retention, access restrictions, and operational cost. A connector configured on paper is not evidence of usable telemetry.

Map priority threats to relevant MITRE ATT&CK techniques, expected signals, detection logic, triage, and response. Identify uncovered paths. Specify who can isolate a teaching server, disable an account, or approve automated containment, particularly during examinations. Include escalation, rollback, and preservation of evidence. [SC-200](SC-200.md) develops analyst workflows; our architectural task is to make those workflows possible within explicit coverage and authority boundaries.

## 6. Translate data obligations into separate controls

Discover and classify public notes, unpublished solutions, personal records, and research material before choosing protection. [Microsoft Purview Information Protection](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/purview/information-protection) supports discovery, classification, sensitivity labels, and protection capabilities. Check supported locations, applications, and licences.

Distinguish a classification result from an applied label, a label from its configured encryption, and DLP from universal prevention of disclosure. Encryption at rest does not stop an authorised application from exporting plaintext. Retention governs preservation and deletion, with separate requirements for legal holds and investigation. Map each obligation to control scope, accountable owner, evidence, and review. Benchmark mappings, compliance scores, and deployed products support assessment; they do not guarantee legal compliance. University legal and privacy owners must interpret obligations.

## 7. Cover hybrid infrastructure and network dependencies

Inventory servers, student devices, containers, SaaS, and laboratory IoT or OT, including unmanaged and unsupported assets. Select posture and workload protection capabilities according to asset risk and supported coverage. Defender for Cloud, Azure Arc, endpoint management, and specialised IoT protection have distinct roles; onboarding one asset class does not establish protection everywhere.

For the notes API, draw ingress, egress, segmentation, private endpoints, DNS resolution, remote administration, and cross-cloud paths. A private endpoint does not automatically disable public access or configure campus DNS. Include certificate and key ownership, rotation, access, and outage behaviour. Prioritise exploitable paths to critical assets above cosmetic improvements to a score, and record compensating controls for legacy systems.

## 8. Include software supply chains and AI boundaries

Threat-model source changes, dependencies, build runners, artefact repositories, deployment identities, APIs, and production releases. Propose review, dependency and secret scanning, provenance checks, protected artefacts, and scoped deployment permissions. Assign responsibility for exceptions and remediation; a scan without an owner leaves a process gap.

The AI assistant adds retrieved documents, model inputs, tool responses, and tool execution. Treat retrieved text as untrusted data. [Prompt Shields](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/openai/concepts/content-filter-prompt-shields) can detect prompt and document attack patterns, but detection is not an authorisation boundary or a guarantee of factual output. Limit retrieval to permitted courses, minimise tool permissions, require approval for consequential actions, and design evaluations using synthetic content. Check the specific integration's supported controls.

## 9. Recover the application and its control plane

Our exercise sets a two-hour recovery time objective (RTO) and fifteen-minute recovery point objective (RPO) for authorised document reads. These are requirements to validate, not product promises. Prioritise identity, DNS, keys, configuration, databases, files, and deployment artefacts needed to restore that operation.

Design protected backups with appropriate isolation, immutability where supported, separate administration, monitoring, and recoverable key dependencies. Replication alone can propagate corruption or malicious deletion. Describe a clean recovery environment and how administrators regain trustworthy control if privileged accounts are compromised. A successful file restore does not prove application recovery. A tabletop should identify restore order, integrity checks, credential recovery, approvals, and how elapsed time and recovered data would be measured.

## 10. Defend a phased implementation with evidence

The university cannot replace every legacy system before next semester. Compare options by risk reduction, cost, licensing, staff capacity, user impact, and recovery complexity. An initial phase might protect privileged access and backups before expanding detection coverage and modernising departmental applications. Dependencies determine sequence.

For each phase, specify a pilot boundary, owner, acceptance evidence, rollback condition, and an expiring exception approved by the risk owner. Compare expected control coverage before and after migration; avoid equating procurement with effectiveness. Finish with an ADR explaining the chosen path and remaining risks, then use the [cases](SC-100-cases.md) and [workbook](SC-100-workbook.md) to challenge it. Revisit the official guide to identify remaining study gaps.
